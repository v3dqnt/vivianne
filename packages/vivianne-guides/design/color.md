# Colour

## The system

Every document has:

- **ink** — body text, near-black, never pure black on white (`#18181B`, warm: `#1C1917`).
- **muted** — secondary text: captions, labels, metadata (`#6B6B74`).
- **faint** — the quietest marks: running heads, list markers (`#A1A1AA`).
- **paper** — the page (`#FFFFFF`; dark: `#0E1116`).
- **surface** — a barely-there fill for code and callouts (`#F5F5F6`).
- **line** — hairlines (`#E4E4E7`).
- **accent** — one colour, used sparingly.

Pick warm neutrals (stone) with warm accents and serif type; cool neutrals (zinc) with cool accents and sans type.

## The accent

Use it for at most a few things per page: links, the lead series of a chart, a small eyebrow label, a section number, a 1–2 pt rule. Not headings, not table headers, not backgrounds of large areas.

Good accents are slightly muted, not neon: indigo `#3451D1`, brick `#A6402B`, navy `#1F3A5F`, forest `#2F6B4F`, burnt orange `#B4532A`. On dark pages, lighten the accent (`#8EA2FF`).

## Brand colours

When the user has a brand colour, use it as the accent, and keep everything else neutral. If the brand colour is very bright or light, use it for marks and charts, not for text. Check contrast.

## Contrast

- Body text against its background: at least 7:1 (WCAG AAA). Muted text: at least 4.5:1.
- Never put text on photos without a solid or darkened area behind it.
- Don't rely on colour alone: label chart series, and give status a word ("Risk", "Done"), not just a colour.

## Dark pages

Good for decks shown on screens and for posters; rarely for printed documents. Use off-black (`#0E1116`), off-white text (`#F4F4F5`), slightly brighter accent, and thinner, dimmer rules (`#2A2F38`).

## Status colours

Only for callouts or states, and only in a small label: success `#2F7D4F`, warning `#A1590B`, danger `#B42318`, info = the accent.
