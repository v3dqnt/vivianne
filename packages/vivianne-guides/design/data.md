# Charts and tables

## Choose the right form

| To show | Use |
|---|---|
| Change over time | line (many points), column (few periods) |
| Comparison between items | horizontal bar, sorted by value |
| Part of a whole | stacked bar, or a single big number; pie/donut only for 2–5 parts |
| A few key figures | big numbers (`metrics` / `stats`), not a chart |
| Exact values to look up | a table |
| Relationship | scatter |

Never 3D. Never dual axes unless the reader is technical. Start bar axes at zero.

## Direct attention

- Put the **lead series first**: it gets the accent; comparisons get neutral greys.
- Label lines at their ends instead of a legend when there are 2–4 lines.
- One series of bars: label the values on the bars and drop the gridlines if it's still clear.
- Give the chart a title that says the finding ("Revenue grew every quarter"), not the axes ("Revenue by quarter").
- Units in the title or the axis once ($k, %), not on every label.

## Numbers

- Round to what matters: $4.2M, not $4,213,887.21, unless the exact figure is the point.
- Thousands separators. Consistent decimals within a column.
- Negative numbers with a minus sign (−12%) or in brackets for finance.
- Percent changes with a sign (+18%).

## Tables

- A rule under the header (ink, ~1 pt), hairlines between rows, no vertical lines, no fills.
- Header in small tracked uppercase, muted.
- Numbers right-aligned, header too; text left-aligned.
- Tabular figures.
- Sort rows meaningfully (by size, or by a natural order), not alphabetically by default.
- A total row: bold, above a rule.
- Keep it short; move long tables to an appendix or a spreadsheet.
