# Writing for design

Good design starts with less, better text.

- **Titles say the point.** "Revenue grew 18%, led by EMEA" beats "Q4 results". On slides, the title is the takeaway; the body is the evidence.
- **One idea per paragraph, one point per bullet.** Bullets are fragments, not sentences joined by commas. Five bullets at most; usually three.
- **Cut filler.** "In order to", "it is important to note that", "leverage", "robust", "seamless", "delve" — remove. Say the thing.
- **Numbers over adjectives.** "Activation up 23%" beats "significantly improved activation".
- **Front-load.** Put the key word at the start of a heading, a bullet, a caption.
- **Consistent grammar** within a list: all verbs, or all nouns.
- **Name things the reader's way**, not the system's.
- **Don't decorate with emoji** in professional documents.

Length targets: an executive summary fits on one page; a slide has ≤ 25 words of body; a caption is one sentence; a CV fits one page (two for long careers).
